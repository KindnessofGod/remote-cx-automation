## 0. Business case

**[VENDOR-PUBLIC/INFERRED]** Notice-period math is a real liability surface —
statutory notice varies by country/tenure, and getting it wrong (understating
it to the employee, or letting a shorter-than-statutory date proceed
uncorrected) is a wrongful-termination-adjacent risk even though nothing is
being "terminated" here. Remote's own platform returns the employee's
proposed last working day but performs no computation of what the legally
correct notice period should be — that gap is the actual value-add.

## 2. Trigger & user story
An employee resigns through Remote's own portal — there is no API path for
this automation, or any third party, to originate that action. UC-05 reacts
to the resignation record appearing, computes the legally correct notice
period from the employee's contract/country rules layered on top of Remote's
raw dates, and flags any discrepancy against the employee's proposed last
working day — all before a specialist confirms the math and it reaches the
employee. Employer-initiated terminations are explicitly out of scope
(different, heavier-risk flow).

## 4. Tools
- **REST reads only** — offboarding record, employment, time off. **No REST writes to Remote at all** — there is nothing to write to.
- **Notice-period calculator** (deterministic microservice).
- **Foundation:** `remote-rest-client`, `audit-logger`, `zaf-app`, `identity-service`, `mock-remote-server`. No `schema-validator` needed — no write payload to validate.

## 5. Workflow (n8n)
```
offboarding.submitted webhook (or poll GET /offboardings?type=resignation
  if the webhook doesn't split by type — confirm at build time)
 → n8n → identity verify → filter to type: "resignation"
 → REST: fetch resignation record + employment (seniority date) + time_offs
 → deterministic: statutory notice end-date (tenure + country notice table);
   accrued PTO reconciliation; delta vs. proposed_last_working_day
 → ZAF sidebar: show calculated notice date + PTO figures + any delta
 → specialist confirms the math (or the discrepancy flag) before it is
   communicated to the employee → audit
 → country notice rule not confirmable with high confidence → escalate,
   never guess a formula
```
There is no "on approve → write" step. The specialist's action is
confirming what gets *communicated*, not submitting anything to Remote.

## 6. LLM reasoning scope
**Does:** none required for the core calculation (deterministic from
structured API fields) — an LLM may draft the plain-language explanation of
the notice-period math for the specialist/employee, never compute it.
**Must NOT:** decide the legal notice period itself; assert a country's
statutory rule when the maintained table doesn't have high-confidence
coverage.

## 7. Deterministic logic
- Notice end-date = f(seniority date, tenure, statutory notice table) — never a single hardcoded formula across countries.
- Accrued PTO payout reconciliation from the Time Off API, **×100 money scaling**.
  Built 2026-08-19 (`src/remote/leaveBalances.js`); before that the days came
  from whatever the requester typed into a form. Three rules, each of which cost
  money the other way:
  - **The payout base is Remote's `balance`, READ and never recomputed.**
    `annual_entitlement − used` is a different field (`annual_balance`) and a
    different number: on one live record it is 25 days where `balance` is 13.75.
  - **`days` and `hours` are components of ONE quantity** — `{days: 13, hours: 6}`
    is 13.75 days at that row's own `working_hours_per_day`. 24 of 117 live
    policies carry a remainder; reading `days` alone underpays, quietly.
  - **The pay rate is supplied with the request, never derived.** See §3. A
    balance with no rate is refused at gate 7 naming the rate, and the report
    says in words that no further read of Remote can answer it.
- Discrepancy delta: statutory-computed date vs. `proposed_last_working_day`.
- Country/rule not confirmable → escalate rather than compute with low confidence.
- **"We hold no rule" and "there is no rule" are different findings** and produce
  different outcomes — `unsupported_country` against `no_statutory_notice_period`.
  Neither is a notice period of zero days. See **§7a**.

## 7a. Country coverage, and the argument for a third outcome

**Added 2026-08-20.** The demonstrable country set is **NL · PT · CA · US**
(`docs/DEMO-COUNTRIES.md`). The notice table covered **PT and CA only**, so half
the demo set fell straight through to `unsupported_country`. This section records
what was added, what was deliberately not added, and the one decision in it that
is genuinely arguable.

### The table now holds NL and US, and they were added for different reasons

| | What was added | Source | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NL** | One month, flat, regardless of tenure, ending at the end of a calendar month | BW art. 7:672 lid 4 and lid 1 (`D-01`), with art. 628a via lid 5 (`D-40`) | `[CONFIRMED — statute, retrieved]`, bytes committed and SHA-256 verifiable |
| **US** | The finding that **no statutory minimum notice binds a resigning employee** | WARN, as published by the US DOL (`D-06`) | `[INFERRED — argument from scope]`, bytes committed |
| **PT** | *Correction*: the bracket boundary moved from 23 to 24 months | CT art. 400.º(1) (`D-02`) | `[CONFIRMED]` — `CONTRADICTIONS.md` C-18 |

Three things about NL that a summary of art. 7:672 would have got wrong, and that
only reading the retrieved text caught:

1. **The tenure ladder at the top of the article is the employer's** (lid 2). The
   employee's obligation is lid 4 and has **no tenure dimension at all**. A row
   written from the first numbers in the article models the wrong party — the
   identical mistake this table already made once for Portugal (C-20). Once is a
   mistake; twice would be the method, which is why the warning now lives in the
   code and not only in the corpus.
2. **"One month" is not "30 days."** Under lid 1's end-of-month anchor the two
   diverge by a **full month** whenever notice is filed on the 1st of a 31-day
   month: 30 days lands on the 31st of that same month, which is already a month
   end, so the anchor leaves it alone. One calendar month lands on the 1st of the
   next, whose month end is a month later. Both look plausible on a form. The
   table now states periods in `noticeDays` **or** `noticeMonths` — whichever the
   statute states — and a test pins the divergence.
3. **The month is conditional.** Lid 5 makes the notice **four days** — or 24
   hours under a collective agreement — where the volume of work is not fixed,
   and disapplies the end-of-month anchor entirely. That is recorded as a caveat
   on every Dutch decision (`C-28`) and deliberately **not** built as a gate:
   art. 628a(9)'s test is not answerable from anything on a Remote employment
   record, and branching on `work_hours_per_week` would be a proxy this repo
   invented for a statutory test — the failure UC-03 already paid for live.

### The third outcome, and why a zero would have been the wrong encoding

Three facts were sharing one outcome, and they send a case to three different
desks:

| Outcome | What it says | The work order |
|---|---|---|
| `unsupported_country` | We hold no rule. The law may require anything. | Add the country. |
| **`no_statutory_notice_period`** | Sourced: no statutory minimum binds a resigning employee. What is owed comes from the **contract**, which this system does not hold. | Open the contract. |
| `no_matching_notice_bracket` | We hold the country's rule; no bracket covers this tenure. | Extend the table's low end. |

The distinction is carried by `statutoryMinimumExists` on the calculator's
result, which is **three-valued** — `null` (we hold no rule, so we do not know),
`false` (sourced: none exists), `true` (one exists) — rather than by a second
boolean or by a day count of zero.

**Zero would have been the worst of the available encodings.** `0` is a quantity
a reader can act on, and what it says is *"this employee owes no notice"* — a
claim about their **contract**, which no source behind the US row supports. The
row's content is that the **statute** is silent. Statutory silence is not
contractual silence, and this system has only checked the first. The same
reasoning is why `tenureMonths` is the real figure on this path and not zeroed,
and why a proposed leaving date is reported `not_comparable` rather than `match`:
there is no statutory end to score it against, and `match` would report agreement
with a standard nobody applied.

### Does "no statutory minimum" reach sign-off, or escalate? — **it escalates**

This is the one arguable decision in the pass, so the argument is recorded rather
than the conclusion alone.

**The case for `prepared_for_signoff`:** the answer is *known*, not missing. The
system has a sourced finding and can state it. Escalating every American
resignation arguably re-creates the very collapse this pass was opened to fix,
since the US then routes exactly like an unsupported country.

**Why it escalates anyway — three independent reasons, any one sufficient:**

1. **A sign-off form with a blank notice section invites the worst reading.**
   `prepared_for_signoff` asks a specialist to *confirm the AI's math*. There is
   no math. Gate 8 of this very engine already refuses to advance a report whose
   payout is `null`, for exactly this reason: *"a report reaching HR Ops for
   sign-off with `totalInRemoteInteger: null` invites exactly the reading the
   ×100 invariant exists to prevent — 'no payout due' — when the truth is 'we
   could not work it out'."* A null notice period is the same shape.
2. **The binding document is one this system cannot see.** What a resigning
   American owes comes from their contract. Passing the case to sign-off would
   let a specialist confirm a notice position that nothing in the chain has
   checked against the only document that governs it. That is not a human gate;
   it is a human rubber stamp on an unexamined question.
3. **The evidence is the weakest in the table, and the routing should reflect
   that.** `D-06` is `[INFERRED — argument from scope]`: WARN states what
   employers of one hundred or more owe on mass layoffs and never addresses the
   employee side; state mini-WARN statutes and contractual notice are both
   untouched. An inferred negative is allowed to change what the record **says**;
   it is not allowed to advance a case toward a human signing a figure off. Every
   other row that reaches sign-off is `[CONFIRMED]` against a retrieved statute.

**Why this is not the collapse it superficially resembles.** The thing that was
wrong before was never the *routing* — it was the *recorded reason and the words
a human reads*. Two cases can go to the same desk with completely different work
orders, and this engine already makes that argument for itself at gate 8:
*"Both escalate, so the routing is identical either way and only the RECORDED
REASON differs — and relabelling a real legal discrepancy as a data problem would
send the case to the wrong desk."* An American resignation now arrives saying
*"no statutory minimum applies; read the contract"*, carrying the flag
`contractual_notice_not_held` and a citation that states in its own words what it
is **not** evidence of. That is a materially better answer than *"the United
States is not a country we cover."*

**What would change this decision:** a source establishing the negative directly
rather than by scope, plus a way for the system to see the contractual notice
term. With both, `prepared_for_signoff` becomes defensible. With neither, it is a
rubber stamp.

### 15c. The Time Off read (2026-08-19) — the payout number finally has a source

`docs/use-cases/UC-05.md` §7 had promised "accrued PTO payout reconciliation
from Time Off API" since the spec was written, and
`grep -rn "leave-policies\|timeoff-balances" src/` returned **no consumer**.
The days multiplied into a resignation settlement were whatever the requester
typed. Every other unsourced figure in this system escalates to a human who can
check it; this one had a signature line under it.

**What the live API actually returns** (all 112 Sandbox employments swept,
117 leave policies, every one HTTP 200):

```json
{"data": [{
  "leave_policy": {"name": "Paid time off", "unit": "days",
                   "description": null, "leave_type": "paid_time_off"},
  "annual_entitlement":  {"type": "limited", "hours": 0, "days": 30},
  "current_entitlement": {"type": "limited", "hours": 6, "days": 18},
  "used":                {"type": "limited", "hours": 0, "days": 5},
  "balance":             {"type": "limited", "hours": 6, "days": 13},
  "annual_balance":      {"type": "limited", "hours": 0, "days": 25},
  "taken": …, "booked": …, "pending_approval": …,
  "upcoming_requested": …, "upcoming_approved": …,
  "working_hours_per_day": 8
}]}
```

**Three claims inherited from a handoff note, checked rather than trusted:**

1. *"`balance.days` is the derived remainder (`annual_entitlement − used`)"* —
   **false.** That figure is `annual_balance` (116 of 117 policies, exact).
   `balance` is the accrued-to-date remaining figure: `current_entitlement −
   used` on 108 of 117, and larger still on the other 9 (a carried-over balance
   the API does not decompose). The deprecated `timeoff-balances` endpoint
   corroborates it under the name `remaining`. Re-deriving the base would have
   paid the row above **25 days instead of 13.75**.
2. *"Remote supplies no hourly rate"* — **true, and it is the finding that
   shapes the design.** But the supporting claim that NL/PT EOR records lack
   `work_hours_per_week` is wrong in both directions: NL carries `40` as a
   number, CA carries `"40"` as a string, US omits it entirely, and the PT
   contractor's `contract_details` is `{}`. Even where present, a rate needs a
   weeks-per-year figure Remote never states. So the rate is a **caller-supplied
   input**; a request without one is refused at gate 7, and
   `describeSignoffBasis()` says so in the specialist's own words: *"The accrued
   days themselves came from Remote's Time Off API and are not in doubt — what
   is missing is the pay rate, which Remote does not publish on any endpoint …
   it is not something a further read of Remote can answer."*
3. *"the schema permits `{unlimited}` with no `days` key even though no instance
   exists"* — **an instance exists.** Employment `7b769e51` is an unlimited-PTO
   employee whose `annual_entitlement` and `annual_balance` are exactly
   `{type: "unlimited"}` — while its `balance` is an ordinary `limited` **zero**.
   Reading `balance` alone therefore produces a confident **0.00 final
   settlement** on a policy where "how many days are owed" is a statutory
   question this repo does not hold. `normalizeLeaveBalances()` consults the
   ENTITLEMENT for that reason, and the shape is now a mock fixture rather than
   a defensive branch.

**Also found live, and not previously recorded:** `leave_policy.unit` reads
`"hours"` on 4 policies (the Italian `ex_festivita`/`rol` entitlements) — those
rows still express their balance as the same `{days, hours}` pair, so nothing
keys off `unit`; and **13 employments carry a NEGATIVE balance** (−20 … −1),
**9 of them active US `global_payroll_employee` records**. `unusableFields()`'s
F-33 guard refuses every one by name rather than clamping it to a computable
zero — verified against the real data, not assumed.

**Behaviour change worth knowing about.** A resignation that supplies no
balances now READS them. Four outcomes, each a different fact, all recorded on
the audit row as `ptoSource`: `caller_supplied` (the form's own numbers win, and
are never overridden), `remote_leave_policy_summary`, `not_read` (a client with
no Time Off method — every hand-built fake in the suite, behaviour unchanged),
and `remote_leave_policy_summary_unreadable` (we asked and got no answer — one
refused balance line, so gate 7 escalates rather than settling quietly at
nothing). The currency now comes from `contract_details.compensation_currency_code`
when the caller names none, instead of labelling every settlement USD.

### 15d. Who a shortfall goes to, and what they decide it from (2026-08-19)

**The question this pass answers**, asked of a real escalation — a Portuguese
employee, 85 months' service, proposing a last working day **23 days** inside
the statutory 60:

> *"How exactly will this system help local HR and the Remote CX automation
> team, especially if it is escalated? Who is it escalated to?"*

Two defects. Both were visible on ticket **#53** in the live account, five lines
apart in the same note.

#### 1. The screen named a team that did not exist

`decisionFacts.js` told the reader *"Local HR Legal decides how the shortfall is
handled"* — five occurrences across `src/uc05/` — while
`src/shared/escalationRouting.js` routed **UC-05 → HR Ops**, and no group called
Local HR Legal existed in the routing table, in the Zendesk account, or in
`scripts/setup-zendesk-groups.mjs`.

**Resolved by adding the routing the prose implied, not by find-and-replace**,
because this spec names two owners and always has:

| Path | Owner | Where the spec says so |
|---|---|---|
| `prepared_for_signoff` | **HR Ops** | §15 — "Single HR Ops sign-off"; the signed report is the artifact |
| `escalate` | **Local HR & Legal** | §8 — "Statutory discrepancy or unconfirmable country rule → escalate to Local HR/Legal" |

They are different acts. Signing off is confirming an arithmetic result. A
statutory shortfall is not an arithmetic result to confirm — the arithmetic is
agreed and it is what creates the problem — and Código do Trabalho **art. 401.º**
attaches a money consequence to how it is answered. UC-04 already carries the
same two-owner shape (`escalationGroup`), so this is the second row of an
existing pattern rather than a new mechanism.

**The team name is now derived, never typed.** `escalationTeamFor("UC-05")`
(`src/shared/escalationRouting.js`) is read by both the panel sentence and the
gate ladder's `statutory_discrepancy` rung, and
`test/uc05DecisionSources.test.js` asserts the string appears in **no other file
under `src/`** — the mismatch cannot come back by someone editing one copy.

**One live consequence, stated rather than smoothed over.** `Local HR & Legal`
does **not** exist in the `your-subdomain` account (8 groups today; it is not among
them), so until `scripts/setup-zendesk-groups.mjs` creates it and
`npm run sync-groups` regenerates `src/shared/escalationGroupIds.js`, a UC-05
escalation takes the tag-and-say-so path: tagged, unassigned, with a note naming
the intended team. That is the designed fail-visible behaviour, and the ticket
still carries **`queue_hr_ops`** — the queue tag deliberately does not move —
so HR Ops's own view still finds it meanwhile. It joins `Mobility Specialists`
as the second unprovisioned team on the standing hole
`src/approvalqueue/teams.js` reports.

#### 2. The escalation stated a conflict and offered nothing to resolve it

The panel said: proposed 2026-08-31, statutory 2026-09-23, −23 days, *"a real
conflict between what was proposed and what the law requires"*. All true, all
arithmetic, and **it does not help anyone decide**. The person receiving it is
choosing between real handlings, and the system held the document that speaks to
the choice and never showed it.

**What the statute actually says about a shortfall** (D-02, Código do Trabalho,
retrieved 2026-08-19 — the enacting text plus Lei n.º 13/2023, not a
consolidation):

- **Art. 401.º** — an employee who fails, wholly or partly, to serve the notice
  *"deve pagar ao empregador uma indemnização de valor igual à **retribuição
  base e diuturnidades correspondentes ao período em falta**"*. So a shortfall
  is a money question, not only a date question — and the base it is measured on
  is **not** the pay rate this system uses for the PTO payout.
- **Art. 400.º(6) with art. 401.º(2)** — a worker recognised as a victim of
  domestic violence owes **no notice and no indemnity**. This system cannot see
  that status and must not try to (**C-19**); it is printed so the person
  weighing the shortfall knows the exemption exists before asking the employee
  for anything.
- **Art. 400.º(2)** — a collective instrument or the contract may lengthen the
  notice to as much as six months for administration, direction, representation
  or responsibility roles, and **art. 400.º(3)** gives fixed-term contracts their
  own 30/15-day ladder. Either would change which minimum this shortfall is
  measured against; the table has a field for neither.
- **Silent on** the employer accepting the earlier date, and on an agreed
  alternative date. Reported as *silent*, never as permitted or forbidden —
  both of those would be conclusions this repository has no standing to reach.

Delivered as `src/uc05/decisionSources.js`, following the pattern
`src/uc04/decisionSources.js` established: a **hand-curated** map from finding to
vendored document, repo-relative `path`, no retrieval, no ranking, no similarity
score, and **a finding with no entry gets no citation rather than a nearest
match**. One thing is different and it is the shape that matters here — a UC-05
finding is a finding about a *country's* statute, so the lookup takes the
country. **This corpus holds the notice statute for one of the nine countries in
the table (PT).** The other eight get an explicit *"no source, and here is what
the number does rest on"*, because "we hold nothing for GB" and "GB needs no
source" read identically on a screen.

Nothing in the decision changed: same gates, same `escalate`, same reason, same
flags. What changed is what the escalation carries — `basis.discrepancy.sources`
(D-02, with caveats C-19 and C-18), `basis.discrepancy.handling` (the four
handlings, the exemption, the two contract dimensions), and a new **unknown**
saying in words that **no figure for the shortfall exists anywhere on the case**
and that computing one is not a gap to close in code.

**Not encoded, deliberately:** no indemnity formula, no threshold, no
recommendation, no ordering of the handlings. `test/uc05DecisionSources.test.js`
pins all of it — including that the handling block contains **no number at all**,
that no gate imports the citations, and that the D-02 sidecar really does contain
each article the locators name.

#### A finding for `CONTRADICTIONS.md`, reported rather than filed

Other agents were live in `docs/knowledge/` during this pass, so this is
recorded here for whoever next edits that file rather than written into it:

> **A statutory shortfall is reported as a number of days, and the statute
> attaches a payment to it that nothing in this system mentions.** For Portugal,
> art. 401.º measures an indemnity by the base retribution and diuturnidades for
> the period not served. UC-05's report reaching a human carries a PTO payout —
> a money column — beside "23 days SHORT", and that column accounts for none of
> it. This is now said in words on the case (`decisionFacts.js`'s "What the
> shortfall costs, if anything"); it is **wrong to encode**, for the same reason
> as C-7's 25 % and C-19's waiver: a money figure derived from a statute read
> once, on one day, printed on a document an employee signs.

## 16. Why this result panel carries no offer — and what its dialog does carry (2026-08-20)

**CORRECTED THE SAME DAY, and the heading above changed with it.** The project
owner: *"I want the answer for everything to always be in the pop up, and then
in this case a recommendation."* That does not overturn the rule below — it
corrects its premise. **An answer is not nothing.** It is the one thing the
requester came for, and it was measured below the fold on every result this
portal produces, at both 1180×900 and 340×740 (`UC-03.md` §21 has the fourteen
numbers). So every decided result now opens with a dialog carrying the ANSWER,
and this section's real subject is narrower than its original title: what this
panel has no **offer** to put underneath it.

The reasoning that follows is unchanged and is why that dialog holds an answer
and a way out and nothing else — an interruption carrying a button nobody can
act on is the thing the rule refuses, and it would refuse it just as firmly
inside an answer's dialog as on its own.

The rule is written down once, with the full nine-row table, in
`docs/use-cases/UC-03.md` §19: **a result earns a modal when it offers the
reader a next action they would otherwise have to discover by scrolling.** An
offer interrupts; a hand-off says so in place; a refusal is never a modal. A
modal carrying nothing actionable is worse than a paragraph — it teaches people
to dismiss without reading, and the next thing dismissed without reading is a
disclaimer.

**UC-05 is a hand-off.** HR Ops checks the figures and signs them off, and that signed-off summary IS the record — there is no Remote write endpoint for a termination and the workflow takes no termination-capable client. So nothing on a UC-05 result is the employee's to take up. The panel states the wait; it does not interrupt to state it.

## 17. The panel named a UUID where a person belongs (2026-08-20)

The project owner, reading the live ZAF sidebar: *"In that Zendesk bar I never
even saw any relevant info of the employee — not even name. That is bad."*
`RemoteClient.getEmployment()` normalises a display name onto every employment
it returns and **none of the nine by-ticket views published it** — the stores
persist `employment_id` and nothing about the person.

Both `GET` views now carry **`employee`** (`src/shared/employeeSubject.js`) and
**`requester`** (`src/shared/requesterSubject.js`), in the one shape every use
case publishes them, so the sidebar needs a single renderer. `employee` is read
LIVE when the panel opens — the same read the action on the screen already
performs before it acts — and reports five states rather than a blank:
`available`, `not_found` (404, an answer *about the record*), `unavailable`
(403/5xx, the request was never evaluated), `not_looked_up` and
`no_employment_id`. A failed read degrades the block and never the case.

### The fields, and what was rejected

`full_name`, `status`, `contract_type`, `country_code`, `start_date`.

- **`country_code`** selects the statutory notice table; **`start_date`**
  selects the bracket inside it — and it is the field UC-05 needs the stand-in
  for, because the raw Sandbox returns `undefined` for
  `basic_information.start_date`.
- **`contract_type`** decides whether a statute applies at all.
  `noticePeriodTable.js` is employment law; for a contractor the answer comes
  from the contract, and `decisionFacts.js` already says this system *"does not
  hold contracts and has not read one."*
- **`status`** — the calculation presumes an employment that is ending, not one
  that has already ended.

**Rejected:** `job_title` (no notice table anywhere reads a title) and pay. The
PTO payout figure *is* money and *is* this decision's subject; it is already
published from the row. The employee's salary is a different number no gate here
reads.

### Two clocks, on purpose

These fields are the record **as it is now**. `notice` on the row carries the
country, the tenure and the probation flag **as they were** when the calculation
ran. Both are wanted and neither replaces the other — a signer who sees them
disagree has found something worth stopping for, and nothing in this system
compares them.

`test/employeeSubject.test.js` and `test/requesterSubject.test.js` for the
mechanism.
