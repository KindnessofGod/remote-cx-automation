**[VENDOR-PUBLIC]** Cross-border tax/social-security questions are core EOR
territory — wrong guidance has real financial/legal consequences, and UC-04's

presence-day calculator, disclaimer injection, and dossier compilation — all

## 6. LLM reasoning scope
**Does:** parse inquiries; search treaty indices; draft research dossier for specialists.
**Must NOT:** provide direct tax/legal advice to the customer; assert residency/withholding conclusions; skip the disclaimer.

The dossier narrative (`dossierBuilder.buildDossier()`) is the one piece of
LLM-authored prose in this use case — restating already-computed facts
(presence days, treaty citations) as text, never a source of new ones. It is
the target of the `narrative-judge` component (`00-FOUNDATION.md`
§6): a scoped faithfulness check against those structured inputs, not a
general quality score. **Built (`src/shared/narrativeJudge.js`,
issue #27)** — `judgeNarrative({narrative, structuredInputs})` runs the
same validate-or-fallback DI discipline as `src/uc01/classifier.js`, attaches
its verdict to the dossier for the tax specialist to see alongside the prose,
and is NEVER read by any gate, route, or policy: UC-08's only decision stays
"escalate, always" regardless of the verdict. On any failure (LLM error,
invalid response shape, judge unconfigured) it attaches an explicit
`{verdict: "not_evaluated"}` sentinel rather than fabricating a positive OR
negative verdict — surfaced, never consumed.

## 17. Read as a deliverable, 2026-08-19 — the day count's subject, and the corpus's real scope

Full write-up: `docs/BUILD-LOG.md` §3.73.

**Reader:** a Remote Tax Operations specialist. **Decision:** what guidance to
author, and whether there is a residency / withholding / totalization exposure.
UC-08 has no execution path, so the dossier IS the product.

### A day count with no subject and no records behind it

`computePresenceDays()` is careful in every way that had already been paid for
(union not sum, explicit `NOT_EVALUATED`, named problems). What it returns is:

```
presenceDays: { days: 92, periodsCounted: 1, status: "COUNTED", problems: [] }
```

92 days **in which country**, over **which window**, out of **how many supplied
records**, and what became of the ones that did not count? Every one of those
facts was in `handleTaxInquiry()`'s hand when the count was taken; none reached
the dossier, the stored row, or the API. Beside a citation of the 183-day rule,
that figure cannot be checked, an evidence-based zero cannot be told from an
empty one, and a stay clipped at the window edge is invisible.

`src/uc08/presenceEvidence.js` (new, pure) records the country, the window and
its length, how many records were supplied, and **every** record with either its
clipped contribution (`countedStartDate` / `countedEndDate` / `countedDays`) or
an `excludedBecause` naming why it did not count. A record that vanishes
silently is F-40 — the defect, not the fix.

It is a separate function rather than extra fields on the calculator for two
reasons, the second load-bearing: `workflows/nodes-uc08/buildDossier.js` carries
its own copy and `test/n8nUc08Parity.test.js` compares the two returns with
`deepEqual`, and that file was out of scope. The evidence block is a new dossier
key instead, so both copies still agree on the count. **Rows written by the n8n
path therefore carry no evidence block, and the view says so** — *"the records
this count was taken from were not recorded on this dossier"* — rather than
rendering an empty period list as "no records were supplied", which is a real
and much stronger claim.

### Three general passages reading as jurisdiction-specific authority

`uc08_treaty_citation_vectors` has held **zero rows** for the whole life of this
deployment, so every citation any production run has ever shown was
keyword-matched — and the corpus is three passages of general OECD Model /
totalization principle holding **no bilateral instrument for any pair**. None of
that reached the reader. "OECD Model Tax Convention, Article 4 — Resident
(tie-breaker rules)" arrives looking like the governing text for a DE/ES
question; it is the template the governing text was drafted from.

`describeRetrievalMode()` reads the mode back off the citations' own stated
basis (embedding matches state their rank and threshold; keyword matches quote
the literal keyword) rather than plumbing a second, drift-prone signal.
`citationCoverage` states the corpus size, that it is general principle, that no
bilateral treaty between the named pair was consulted **because none is held**,
and that retrieval was keyword rather than semantic.

**The corpus was NOT expanded.** That is the owner's decision, pending a source
catalogue (`docs/KNOWLEDGE-SOURCES.md`). This is the honest finding made legible,
not the gap closed — and making it legible is what was missing, since the only
record of the limitation was a source comment.

### The 🔴 guarantee, re-verified

`handleTaxInquiry()`'s parameter list still admits no `remote`/`zendesk`/client,
`DossierStore` still has exactly one write method and zero mutations,
`src/uc08/server.js` still has no POST route in the file — all re-run, all still

## 18. Run as a scenario, 2026-08-19 — a day count with no jurisdiction

answer than one with jurisdictions, it is an answer to a different question,
printed in the same shape.** A reader supplies the missing half from memory, and
the half they supply is the 183-day frame the corpus cites — a general
model-convention article, not a rule of any country in play.

2. **Three states, not two** — `no_residence_test_held` (registered, gap

### 19.1 What was built, and why it is a map and not a retriever

`src/uc08/decisionSources.js`, following `src/uc04/decisionSources.js` and
`src/uc05/decisionSources.js`. The distinction from `treatyRetriever.js` is the
whole design and is stated in the file's own header:

> `treatyRetriever.js` **searches** — free text in, whichever of its general
> passages matched, by keyword or by embedding similarity, and `matchedOn` says
> which. It can and does return a nearest match. This file **looks up** — a
> jurisdiction, or a jurisdiction PAIR, resolves to the instrument that governs
> it, or resolves to nothing. **Canada–Portugal is not a near miss for
> Canada–Netherlands**: six instruments, five distinct windows, and one of them
> is not even the same shape. A similarity search that returned one for the
> other would be wrong in a way nothing downstream could detect.

`docs/ESCALATION-DESTINATIONS.md` §8.4 named this as the choice that had to be
made before anything could be built: the corpus is retrieval-shaped and
`decisionSources.js` is map-shaped, and mixing them without deciding which
governs would produce a nearest match for a finding that has no source. The map
governs. The retriever is unchanged and still returns its general passages,
which the dossier now labels as *general principle* beside the instruments in
force.

Resolved at READ time in `dossierView.js`, for the reasons that file's header

## 20. Why this result panel carries no offer — and what its dialog does carry (2026-08-20)

**CORRECTED THE SAME DAY, and the heading above changed with it.** The project
owner: *"I want the answer for everything to always be in the pop up, and then
in this case a recommendation."* That does not overturn the rule below — it
corrects its premise. **An answer is not nothing.** It is the one thing the
requester came for, and it was measured below the fold on every result this
portal produces, at both 1180×900 and 340×740 (`UC-03.md` §21 has the fourteen
numbers). So every decided result now opens with a dialog carrying the ANSWER,
and this section's real subject is narrower than its original title: what this
panel has no **offer** to put underneath it.

The reasoning that follows is unchanged and is why that dialog holds an answer
and a way out and nothing else — an interruption carrying a button nobody can
act on is the thing the rule refuses, and it would refuse it just as firmly
inside an answer's dialog as on its own.

The rule is written down once, with the full nine-row table, in
`docs/use-cases/UC-03.md` §19: **a result earns a modal when it offers the
reader a next action they would otherwise have to discover by scrolling.** An
offer interrupts; a hand-off says so in place; a refusal is never a modal. A
modal carrying nothing actionable is worse than a paragraph — it teaches people
to dismiss without reading, and the next thing dismissed without reading is a
disclaimer.

**UC-08 is a hand-off, permanently.** Same guarantee, same reason as UC-07 — `handleTaxInquiry()` has no write-capable parameter and the graph has no branch. A tax specialist gives the answer; the dossier is the whole outcome. Verified in a browser that the result renders no dialog (`portal-uc08-handoff-no-modal.png`).
