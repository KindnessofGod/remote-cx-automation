# UC-07 — Global Mobility / Permanent Relocation Compliance

> Inherits `00-FOUNDATION.md`. **Architecture overrides both raw docs** (`UC-07 Build Pack By Chatgpt`'s Saga/compensating-transaction engine and v1's Four-Tier + 5-AI-agent execution model) — both proposed autonomous or semi-autonomous *execution* gated only by state checks, contradicting this project's own 🔴 High-tier definition. Full reasoning: `docs/verification/uc07-business-case.md` + tracking issue #8.

## 0. Business case

Full writeup: `docs/verification/uc07-business-case.md`. **[PROPOSED —
reasoned hypothesis]** Permanent cross-country relocation requires ending one
legal employment relationship and starting another through a different
Remote entity — two separate legal actions that must be sequenced so the
employee isn't left without valid employment status, benefits, or income
continuity in the gap. **Cost mechanism**: not primarily wasted support
hours — it's severe harm to a real person's legal employment/immigration/
income status if sequencing goes wrong, at a **permanent** (not temporary)
scale, raising every stake UC-04's research already found for temporary
workations. **[INFERRED]**: permanent relocation is plausibly low-frequency
per employee (people move countries far less often than they travel) —
reasoned from base rates, not sourced. **[UNCONFIRMED]**: actual ticket
volume — very plausibly a low-double-digit internal caseload, not
knowable from outside.

**This business case is why the architecture decision below isn't primarily
a safety argument — it's a cost/benefit one.** Given genuinely low plausible
volume + genuinely severe downside-per-error, building a saga/compensating-
transaction engine or a 5-agent orchestration system to save time on a
process where the human's judgment at each step is what actually matters is
a bad trade before safety even enters as a value statement. **Ships**: an
AI-compiled dossier (§5) — the ROI is specialist research time saved per
case, not ticket-volume deflection, a different economics claim from UC-06.
**Does not ship, not deferred but rejected on the numbers**: any version of
either raw doc's execution engine, at any confirmed volume — the
downside-per-error is severe enough that no plausible volume number changes
the conclusion.

## 2. Trigger & user story
Ending one legal employment and starting another across two jurisdictions
carries a real risk of an employment-status gap if sequencing has a bug.
Today this needs multi-disciplinary research across immigration, local
labor law, tax-treaty nexus, and benefits continuity — real specialist time,
none of which requires the automation to hold write credentials. UC-07
compiles that research into one dossier; the specialist reviews it and
executes every step themselves through Remote's own system.

## 4. Tools
- **REST reads only** — employment, contract-amendments, offboardings, legal entities, countries, cost calculator. **No REST writes.** The dossier-building function takes no write-capable REST or Zendesk client as a parameter at all — the same structural pattern as UC-08's `handleTaxInquiry()`, so there is no execution path to wire up by accident later.
- **RAG retriever** — coverage/risk dimensions, same maintained-table discipline as UC-04, not fuzzy legal retrieval.
- **Foundation:** `remote-rest-client` (reads only), `rag-retriever`, `disclaimer-injector`, `audit-logger`, `zaf-app`, `mock-remote-server`.

## 6. LLM reasoning scope
**Does:** extract request specifics from free text; cross-reference RAG
coverage tables; compile the dossier narrative; draft the pre-filled
paperwork fields from already-fetched/already-decided facts; flag PE/
coverage uncertainty.
**Must NOT:** execute any transfer step; assert legal conclusions as fact;
decide whether the relocation should proceed; submit the drafted paperwork.

### 17.1 What was built

`src/uc07/decisionSources.js` — the same shape and the same discipline as
`src/uc04/decisionSources.js` and `src/uc05/decisionSources.js`: **a hand-curated
map from finding key to the documents somebody read.** Not a retriever. The
distinction is load-bearing and is stated in the file's own header:
`mobilityRetriever.js` SEARCHES and can return a nearest match; this LOOKS UP a
destination or a route and resolves to the instrument that governs it or to
nothing at all. Portugal's D8 is not a near miss for Canada's IRPR s. 186.

Two axes, because a relocation finding is never free-floating:

- **Destination.** Right to work is a question the destination answers and the
  source country has no say in. `IMMIGRATION_ROUTES` holds **PT, CA and US**.
- **Route.** Tax residence and social-security membership are questions about a
  PAIR — and about both ends of it at once, which is what makes them different
  from UC-04's single-destination workation question. All six demo pairs are
  held.

`src/uc07/dossierView.js` resolves them at READ time and exposes `sources`,
`uncited` and a `basis` block. Read-time for the three reasons that file's
header already gives: it applies identically to rows the n8n path wrote, to every
row already in `uc07_dossiers`, and — decisively — `buildDossier()` and
`evaluateRelocationFeasibility()` are deep-compared against
`workflows/nodes-uc07/relocationGates.js` by `test/n8nUc07Parity.test.js`, so
adding a key to either would have broken a comparison rather than improved a
dossier.

### 17.2 The finding that only appeared while wiring it up

**UC-07 has no nationality field.** `grep -rn "nationality\|citizen\|passport"
src/uc07/` matches nothing at all.

Every immigration instrument in this corpus is a destination's published route
for a **third-country national**. Whether Portugal's D8 is this employee's route
— or whether they need any route, as an EU national moving inside the EU would
not — turns entirely on a passport this system does not hold and cannot infer.
Printing "Portugal — residence visa for remote work" without saying that hands a
specialist a route as though it were *this person's* route.

So `NATIONALITY_NOT_HELD` is attached to every immigration citation and raises a
**priority-1** open question, and it is **unconditional** for the same reason
UC-08's `CITIZENSHIP_BASED_TAXATION` is: there is no input to condition on, so
disclosing it only where a visa looked necessary would rebuild the silence for
every case the absence of a field made invisible. A test asserts that no file in
`src/uc07/` outside the two citation modules mentions a nationality — the day one
appears, that constant is stale and has to be revisited rather than left
standing.

### 17.3 What the dossier now says, on the demo set

An **NL→PT** relocation with visa support outstanding (`verdict: BLOCK`) is
handed, in order:

- **Lei n.º 23/2007 art. 61.º-B**, quoted in Portuguese with a marked working
  translation, and — the part that matters — **three explicit silences**: the
  article that creates the D8 contains *no income floor, no maximum duration and
  no insurance requirement*. That is `CONTRADICTIONS.md` **C-17**, printed where
  the decision happens.
- **Art. 54.º(1)(i)**, the second route, carrying the only duration the statute
  states — **less than one year**, which is by definition not a permanent
  relocation. **C-15**: two instruments, not one country-level route.
- **The consular pages (D-11)** for the figure the statute does not contain:
  *"four monthly minimum guaranty remuneration"*. `UC07_SALARY_BELOW_VISA_MINIMUM`
  resolves to **D-11 alone** — citing art. 61.º-B for a euro figure would cite a
  provision that does not contain one, and a test pins that it never does.
- **Both ends of the tax move**: AWR art. 4 (no day count exists in Dutch
  domestic law at all) against CIRS art. 16.º (a 183 line anchored on the tax
  year, *plus* a count-free dwelling limb, *plus* back-dating to the first day of
  the stay). Different shapes, so the day residence ends at one end and begins at
  the other are decided by different kinds of rule — they can overlap or leave a
  gap. **C-12**.
- **The NL–PT convention (D-24)**, cited as an instrument that exists, at a
  named path, with `readHere: false` and a sentence saying its residence article
  has **not been read here** — the sidecar quotes only the employment-income
  article, and that article addresses short stays, not a permanent move.
- **Reg. 883/2004 arts. 11(1) and 11(3)(a)**, quoted — and the sentence the
  block exists for: *every* maximum in this corpus (the EU's 24 months, the US
  5 years, Canada's 24 or 60) is an **exception for a temporary assignment**. A
  permanent move has no anticipated end, so the maxima are not the question and
  the default is the state where the work is pursued. A reader who goes looking
  for a detachment limit will find one and it will be the wrong answer.

A **US→CA** relocation gets IRPR s. 187(3) quoted with its own SI/97-5
reproduction notice, and the observation the regulation's own words support and
this system refuses to conclude from: the test turns on the **payer and the place
of business remaining outside Canada**, and a permanent relocation moves the
payer *into* Canada. Whether that ends the exemption is the specialist's reading;
the dossier states the direction and stops. **C-5**.

A **CA→NL** relocation gets **no immigration citation at all** and an explicit
absence: *"This repository holds an immigration instrument for CA, PT, US only,
and NL is not among them … Another country's visa route is not a substitute for a
missing one."* That is the honest half of the corpus and it is asserted by test.

## 18. Why this result panel carries no offer — and what its dialog does carry (2026-08-20)

**CORRECTED THE SAME DAY, and the heading above changed with it.** The project
owner: *"I want the answer for everything to always be in the pop up, and then
in this case a recommendation."* That does not overturn the rule below — it
corrects its premise. **An answer is not nothing.** It is the one thing the
requester came for, and it was measured below the fold on every result this
portal produces, at both 1180×900 and 340×740 (`UC-03.md` §21 has the fourteen
numbers). So every decided result now opens with a dialog carrying the ANSWER,
and this section's real subject is narrower than its original title: what this
panel has no **offer** to put underneath it.

The reasoning that follows is unchanged and is why that dialog holds an answer
and a way out and nothing else — an interruption carrying a button nobody can
act on is the thing the rule refuses, and it would refuse it just as firmly
inside an answer's dialog as on its own.

The rule is written down once, with the full nine-row table, in
`docs/use-cases/UC-03.md` §19: **a result earns a modal when it offers the
reader a next action they would otherwise have to discover by scrolling.** An
offer interrupts; a hand-off says so in place; a refusal is never a modal. A
modal carrying nothing actionable is worse than a paragraph — it teaches people
to dismiss without reading, and the next thing dismissed without reading is a
disclaimer.

**UC-07 is a hand-off, permanently.** This is the 🔴 guarantee stated as a UI rule. `handleRelocationReview()` takes no write-capable client, the store has one write method and zero mutations, and the API has no POST route in the file — so there is no action for anyone to take on this screen or on any other. **An offer here would be a lie with a button on it.** The panel says the honest thing instead, and says it in place: nothing here approves this request, and nothing anywhere does.
