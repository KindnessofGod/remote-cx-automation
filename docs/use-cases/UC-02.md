## 0. Business case

**[PROPOSED — reasoned hypothesis, not sourced fact]** Expense review is
manual, repetitive admin work — inspect a receipt image, re-check the math,
confirm the category, decide approve/decline — the kind of task industry-wide
that scales badly with headcount and where errors (duplicate reimbursement,
math mistakes, misapplied category) are cheap to prevent deterministically but
expensive to catch after the fact. **Cost mechanism**: Finance Ops time per
claim, plus the real-money risk of a duplicate or miscalculated reimbursement
slipping through.

## 7. Deterministic logic

> **The line-sum check is gone, and what replaced it is the substance of this
> section.** The original spec said "line items sum to stated total". Remote's
> expense model **has no line items** [CONFIRMED] — so that gate could never
> pass, and for months every real claim stopped at `missing_itemized_breakdown`
> while every fail-closed test stayed green. Deleting it was not an option: a
> gate that cannot fail is worse than no gate, because it looks like protection.
> What the line sum was *for* was refusing to auto-approve a stated money figure
> that nothing independent corroborated. The real record still supports exactly
> that, on the quantities it actually has:

| Check | What it compares | Can it fail? |
|---|---|---|
| **Receipt evidence** (replaces "missing itemized breakdown") | `receipts[]` must hold at least one row with a real `id`, `name`, `type` and `inserted_at`. The receipt is the record's **only** independent artifact — the document the money claim rests on. This use case is named "Expense & **Receipt** Validation"; a receiptless auto-approval is the one thing it must never do. | **Yes** — `maxItems: 5` means an empty array is representable, and rows can be malformed. [CONFIRMED the array exists; the empty case is [INFERRED] — all 220 live rows carry exactly one receipt] |
| **Tax containment** (replaces the math check) | `0 ≤ tax_amount ≤ amount`, and the same for the converted pair. The schema defines `tax_amount` as "the tax portion **of** the expense amount" — a portion cannot exceed its whole, and nothing upstream enforces it since both are separately submitted integers. | **Yes** [CONFIRMED semantics] |
| **Conversion identity** (the closest structural analogue to the line sum) | When `currency.code === converted_currency.code` no conversion happened, so `converted_amount` **must** equal `amount` and `converted_tax_amount` must equal `tax_amount`. Four independently stored integers that have to reconcile exactly — two figures that must agree, where disagreement means the record contradicts itself. | **Yes** [CONFIRMED — all 220 live rows agree, which is what a *passing* control looks like on clean data] |
| **Cross-currency: refused, not estimated** | The record carries **no `conversion_rate` field** [CONFIRMED — not in the schema], so given only `amount` and `converted_amount` in different currencies, *any* converted figure is consistent with *some* rate. There is nothing to verify. The tax pair could in principle imply the same rate, but `tax_amount` is tiny (10 minor units on every live row against amounts up to 1,138,127) and integer rounding swamps the ratio — that test would be noise dressed as a control. So every cross-currency claim goes to a human (`currency_conversion_unverified`), which is what §9's "flag estimation vs. final rate" already asked for. | n/a — it always refuses, and says why |

- **Money is an integer in the currency's minor unit** on every amount in/out [CONFIRMED].
- **The policy cap is compared against `converted_amount`, in a declared currency.** `amount` is in the claim's *original* currency and the live account bills in five (EUR/USD/CAD/SGD/GBP), so measuring every claim against one flat cap table silently applied a USD cap to GBP pence. `converted_amount` is defined as the amount "converted to the company's billing currency" — the only figure one cap table can mean anything against. When the billing currency is not the corpus's (`POLICY_CAP_CURRENCY`), the cap is **not applicable** and a human decides (`policy_cap_currency_mismatch`) rather than a wrong comparison being made. [PROPOSED — the caps and their currency are mock policy data; the *axis* is [CONFIRMED]]
- **`expense_date` must be in the past** [CONFIRMED — the schema says so]; a claim for money not yet spent is not auto-reimbursed.
- Duplicate detection via the submitter's receipt **hash** when present, else a server-derived fingerprint over `(employment_id, amount, currency.code, tax_amount, expense_date, title)` — the signature of the same real-world claim filed twice.

### VAT: from a runtime flag to a structural guarantee
§9 requires "never auto-assert VAT recovery". The old gate refused any claim
whose `vat_amount` was non-zero — **a field that does not exist**. The real
field is `tax_amount`, and **every one of the 220 live records carries a
non-zero one**, so mapping the old rule onto it would refuse 100% of real
claims and leave this use case exactly as dead as the `lines` bug did.

The control is preserved **by construction** instead: the approve write's body
is a closed `oneOf` over `{status:"approved"}` and `{status:"declined", reason}`.
There is no VAT-recovery field on the record or in the write, so this
automation *structurally cannot* assert VAT recovery — the same move UC-08
makes by not accepting a write-capable client. What remains genuinely checkable
about tax is its arithmetic containment, above. A test pins that the write body
is exactly `{status:"approved"}` and nothing else. [CONFIRMED]

## 8. Human control points
- OCR/classification confidence < 85% → Finance Ops review.
- Amount over policy cap → HITL.
- **Missing receipt evidence → HITL** (replaces "missing itemized breakdown", which named a field Remote does not have — see §7).
- Record contradicts itself (`tax_exceeds_amount`, `conversion_identity_mismatch`) → HITL.
- Cross-currency claim, or a billing currency the cap corpus is not denominated in → HITL.
- Future-dated `expense_date` → HITL.

## 9. Failure modes & fallbacks
| Failure | Guard |
|---|---|
| Hallucinated total | Deterministic math re-check; low-conf → human |
| Duplicate submission | Hash dedupe across history |
| Misclassified non-English receipt | Confidence gate → review |
| VAT misrecovery | Never auto-assert; flag for Finance |

Failed check → annotate + route to Finance Ops queue.

## 16. Why this result panel carries no offer — and what its dialog does carry (2026-08-20)

**CORRECTED THE SAME DAY, and the heading above changed with it.** The project
owner: *"I want the answer for everything to always be in the pop up, and then
in this case a recommendation."* That does not overturn the rule below — it
corrects its premise. **An answer is not nothing.** It is the one thing the
requester came for, and it was measured below the fold on every result this
portal produces, at both 1180×900 and 340×740 (`UC-03.md` §21 has the fourteen
numbers). So every decided result now opens with a dialog carrying the ANSWER,
and this section's real subject is narrower than its original title: what this
panel has no **offer** to put underneath it.

The reasoning that follows is unchanged and is why that dialog holds an answer
and a way out and nothing else — an interruption carrying a button nobody can
act on is the thing the rule refuses, and it would refuse it just as firmly
inside an answer's dialog as on its own.

The rule is written down once, with the full nine-row table, in
`docs/use-cases/UC-03.md` §19: **a result earns a modal when it offers the
reader a next action they would otherwise have to discover by scrolling.** An
offer interrupts; a hand-off says so in place; a refusal is never a modal. A
modal carrying nothing actionable is worse than a paragraph — it teaches people
to dismiss without reading, and the next thing dismissed without reading is a
disclaimer.

**UC-02 is a hand-off — or, on the happy path, a result with nothing left to do.** A claim that passes every gate is settled at Remote with nobody reading it — there is no next action to offer, because the request is finished. A flagged claim goes to Finance Ops and the requester's next step is to wait; the panel says so in `nextStep()` and points at "My requests". The one action this use case does offer the reader — **"File this as a new claim"**, which mints a fresh copy of a fixture expense — is on the FORM, where a person deciding what to submit is looking, and not on a result that has already been decided.

## 17. The panel named a UUID where a person belongs (2026-08-20)

The project owner, reading the live ZAF sidebar: *"In that Zendesk bar I never
even saw any relevant info of the employee — not even name. That is bad."*
`RemoteClient.getEmployment()` normalises a display name onto every employment
it returns and **none of the nine by-ticket views published it** — the stores
persist `employment_id` and nothing about the person.

Both `GET` views now carry **`employee`** (`src/shared/employeeSubject.js`) and
**`requester`** (`src/shared/requesterSubject.js`), in the one shape every use
case publishes them, so the sidebar needs a single renderer. `employee` is read
LIVE when the panel opens — the same read the action on the screen already
performs before it acts — and reports five states rather than a blank:
`available`, `not_found` (404, an answer *about the record*), `unavailable`
(403/5xx, the request was never evaluated), `not_looked_up` and
`no_employment_id`. A failed read degrades the block and never the case.

### The fields, and what was rejected

`full_name`, `status`, `country_code`.

- **`full_name`** — who is being reimbursed.
- **`status`** — reimbursing someone who has left is the failure nothing else on
  this view could show.
- **`country_code`** — UC-02's category caps **are** per-country:
  `listExpenseCategories()` returns 36 / 33 / 32 / 32 different rows for
  NLD / PRT / CAN / USA, so the country is what makes *"over the cap"* a
  meaningful sentence at all.

**Rejected:** `job_title` and `start_date` (no gate reads either; a claim's
legitimacy does not turn on seniority) and `contract_type` — arguable, since
contractors usually invoice rather than claim, but no gate reads it either, and
a field on a screen that nothing compares is how a reader comes to believe a
check happened.

**Money:** the claimed amount, the cap, the overage and the percentage are
already published as `decisionFacts` — they are the *subject* of this decision.
The employee's own pay is not, and the shared registry has no field for it.

### `requester` reports `not_recorded`, and that is the right answer

`expenseStore.js` has no requester column **by design**: an expense belongs to
the employee who incurred it, and the identity gate's entire job is to confirm
the submitter *is* that employee. Copying the employment id into the slot would
manufacture a second party that does not exist, so the block says so instead.

**Tests:** `test/employeeSubjectAcrossUseCases.test.js`, plus
`test/employeeSubject.test.js` and `test/requesterSubject.test.js` for the
mechanism.
