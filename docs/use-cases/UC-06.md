## 0. Business case

Full writeup: `docs/verification/uc06-business-case.md`. Summary: **keep
UC-06**, reframed away from unverifiable "biggest support bottleneck" claims.
Two confirmed cost mechanisms, not assumed: (1) the 5th-of-month payroll
cutoff is real and customer-facing (Remote's own support content) — a missed
cutoff generates a support contact and a disproportionate trust cost, because
payroll errors read as platform-integrity issues; (2) Remote's own product
team already built self-service amendment automation (the `automatable`
pre-check below), evidence the underlying problem is real even without a
ticket-volume number. **[UNCONFIRMED]**: ticket volume, whether this is a
top-N bottleneck relative to other categories, churn/CSAT correlation.
**Ships regardless**: the cutoff-deadline lookup (near-zero cost, prevents a
specific documented failure mode) and the `automatable` pre-check gate (defers
to a decision Remote's compliance engine already makes, near-zero cost
regardless of how often it fires). **Gated on real data**: further investment
in the dual-control workflow itself (Slack alerts, richer UI, SLA tracking) —
genuinely useful on risk-mitigation grounds, but further investment should
wait for real volume/escalation-rate numbers, not be assumed.

**Why the cutoff itself is real, not an arbitrary internal buffer**: researched
directly (`docs/verification/uc06-payroll-cutoff-timing.md`), against the
broader payroll/EOR industry and government sources, not just Remote's own
docs. Every provider that publishes anything on the subject (Remote, Deel,
ADP, Gusto) enforces *some* pre-pay-date deadline — `[CONFIRMED]` as
industry-standard practice, though no external standard sets the specific
calendar day. Real, externally-anchored mechanics justify a buffer of *some*
length: gross-to-net recalculation, IRS deposit-schedule rules, NACHA
ACH-settlement windows, and the UK's on-or-before-payday RTI filing
requirement all consume real time no processor controls. Deel's own docs
show cutoffs genuinely varying by outside local-processing partner (evidence
a cutoff CAN be partner-driven); Remote's own marketing claims wholly-owned
entities with no partner layer, which points its own cutoff more toward
"coordinating one internal payroll engine across ~100 countries as one
batch" than "waiting on an outside partner" — `[INFERRED]`, since Remote
doesn't publish its own reasoning. **Exactly why "the 5th" specifically
(rather than some other nearby date) stays `[UNCONFIRMED]`** — no source
audits that number, and this project does not manufacture one. Customer-facing
copy should say "this is a real deadline because of how payroll processing
genuinely works," never "the law requires this exact date" — no statute
found anywhere names a specific day.

## 6. LLM reasoning scope
**Does:** parse unstructured change requests; draft standardized amendment summary for employee preview.
**Must NOT:** submit amendments; bypass either approval; skip cutoff/schema/money checks.

`draftSummary()`'s output is the one piece of LLM-authored prose in this use
case — a plain-language restatement of already-decided values, never a
source of new facts (§0/§5). It is the target of the `narrative-judge`
component (`00-FOUNDATION.md` §6): a scoped faithfulness check against the
structured amendment values it was given, not a general quality score.
**Built (`src/shared/narrativeJudge.js`, issue #27)** —
`judgeNarrative({narrative, structuredInputs})` attaches its verdict to the
amendment record for the specialist to see alongside the summary, and is
NEVER read by any gate or policy: `policyEngine.js`'s decision, the
`dual_approval_required` outcome, and `dualApprovalPolicy.js`'s approval
actionability are all unchanged regardless of the verdict. On any failure
(LLM error, invalid response shape, judge unconfigured) it attaches an
explicit `{verdict: "not_evaluated"}` sentinel rather than fabricating a
positive OR negative verdict — surfaced, never consumed.

**On what this sends to OpenAI, precisely.** `draftSummary()`'s prompt
(`src/uc06/changeParser.js:87`) includes the real salary/job-title/hours
change amounts. This is **not a PII exposure** — the payload carries no name
or employment ID, so nothing here is personally identifiable in the
regulatory sense. It is confidential financial data reaching a subprocessor,
and the relevant control is OpenAI's own documented policy, verified live
against `developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data`: API data is not
used for model training by default, is retained up to 30 days for abuse
monitoring, and Zero Data Retention is available for approved enterprise
customers. The one real, narrower gap: `reasonText` is free text and, unlike
`changes`, isn't guaranteed to stay clean of a name if the requester typed
one in. See `docs/adr/0009-sensitive-data-to-llm-policy.md` and tracking
issue #21.

## 8. Human control points
- **Dual control gate:** the **employer's signatory** and a **Remote Payroll specialist** must approve — two people on two sides of the relationship, and **neither of them the person who filed the request**. It applies specifically to the non-automatable path (the subset Remote's own engine already flagged as needing review), exactly where a segregation-of-duties control has real value. **This is this project's own risk-management addition, not sourced from any raw UC-06 doc** — documented explicitly as such rather than presented as a Remote-derived requirement.

  > **CORRECTED 2026-08-21 (DRIFT-098, `[A-1]` `[A-2]` `[A-3]`).** This bullet used to say *"Customer Admin **and** Remote Payroll specialist"*, and the code implements it as the **requesting** admin: `requester` is captured (`src/uc06/workflow.js:241`) and persisted (`amendmentStore.js:40`) and **never compared to either approver**. So the person who typed the salary change can sign the slot confirming they typed it, and slot 1 can never disagree with its own author. The exemption is deliberate and is argued in `src/uc06/dualApprovalPolicy.js:13–29` — **in a code comment, and nowhere in `docs/adr/0005-…`, which exists to argue exactly this and says "two independent people".** UC-01 holds `self_approval`; UC-09 holds requester ≠ approver ≠ payment_releaser. **UC-06 is the only one of the three that exempts itself.**
  >
  > Slot 1 is now the **employer's signature**, in Remote's own vocabulary (`awaiting_employer_signature` / `employer_signed_at`), filled by the company's signing representative — `src/remoteui/roles.js:24` already models that person as *"distinct from the admin USER who operates the console"*. The requester is refused with a **distinct** code, `requester_cannot_approve`, never `same_person_cannot_fill_both_roles`: one says *you already signed the other slot*, the other says *you asked for this*, and they are different afternoons of work for whoever triages them.
  >
  > **`[A-1]` must land before `[A-2]`.** Migrating a role name inside a control change leaves the control down for the duration, and a half-renamed role fails open in the one place that must fail closed.

## 16. Roles in the Remote UI stand-in (issue #34)

The stand-in models three genuinely separate actors, each with its own
server-side session and its own permitted submissions. The role comes from the
authenticated session (the page's role tabs send a session key as a header),
**never a claim in the request body** — a POST that names its own role proves
nothing (CLAUDE.md prime directive #3). The authorization matrix is pure
policy in `src/remoteui/roles.js`; the server applies it and refuses anything
outside the role with a clear error.

| Role | Authenticated session | May submit | May NOT |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Company admin** | `admin_jane` (customer admin) | An amendment **request** (`POST /api/submit`) — the UC-06 trigger (§1/§2 primary actor) | Consent to an amendment (their control point is the dual **approval** in the ZAF sidebar, §8, not a signature) |
| **Employer** | `company_owner` (company representative) | **Consent** on the company's behalf (`POST /api/consent`, party `employer`), for its own employees only | Request an amendment (requesting is reserved to the company admin) or consent as the employee |
| **Employee** | `emp_active_001` (Amara Okafor) | **Consent** to an amendment of her own contract (`POST /api/consent`, party `employee`) | Request an amendment, consent on the employer's behalf, or consent to anyone else's contract |

The rules are grounded in this use case's own sources, not invented: the
customer admin initiates amendments (`§1`/`§2`; the v1 research actor table —
"Customer Admin — Initiates amendment" — and its "Only the company owner and
super admins can submit and view" rule); the employee reviews/signs their own
amendment and the employer signs on the company's behalf (v1's
`awaiting_employee_signature` / `awaiting_employer_signature` signature
states). Consent is recorded through the real surfaces the repo has — a
durable `audit_log` row first (`amendment_employee_consented` /
`amendment_employer_consented`), then a best-effort internal note on the
amendment's Zendesk ticket — and is a stand-in demonstration of the signature
stage, not a new gate in the approval pipeline. See `docs/BUILD-LOG.md` §3.19
and the decision-log entry for the full reasoning.

## 17. Why this result panel carries no offer — and what its dialog does carry (2026-08-20)

**CORRECTED THE SAME DAY, and the heading above changed with it.** The project
owner: *"I want the answer for everything to always be in the pop up, and then
in this case a recommendation."* That does not overturn the rule below — it
corrects its premise. **An answer is not nothing.** It is the one thing the
requester came for, and it was measured below the fold on every result this
portal produces, at both 1180×900 and 340×740 (`UC-03.md` §21 has the fourteen
numbers). So every decided result now opens with a dialog carrying the ANSWER,
and this section's real subject is narrower than its original title: what this
panel has no **offer** to put underneath it.

The reasoning that follows is unchanged and is why that dialog holds an answer
and a way out and nothing else — an interruption carrying a button nobody can
act on is the thing the rule refuses, and it would refuse it just as firmly
inside an answer's dialog as on its own.

The rule is written down once, with the full nine-row table, in
`docs/use-cases/UC-03.md` §19: **a result earns a modal when it offers the
reader a next action they would otherwise have to discover by scrolling.** An
offer interrupts; a hand-off says so in place; a refusal is never a modal. A
modal carrying nothing actionable is worse than a paragraph — it teaches people
to dismiss without reading, and the next thing dismissed without reading is a
disclaimer.

**UC-06 is a hand-off, and it is not on the portal at all** — its entry point is the Remote UI stand-in, `src/remoteui/`. Its result is a hand-off in the strongest sense this repo has: **dual** approval, in the ZAF sidebar, by two named roles neither of which is the requester. There is nothing here for the requester to do next but wait.

## 18. The panel named a UUID where a person belongs (2026-08-20)

The project owner, reading the live ZAF sidebar: *"In that Zendesk bar I never
even saw any relevant info of the employee — not even name. That is bad."*
`RemoteClient.getEmployment()` normalises a display name onto every employment
it returns and **none of the nine by-ticket views published it** — the stores
persist `employment_id` and nothing about the person.

Both `GET` views now carry **`employee`** (`src/shared/employeeSubject.js`) and
**`requester`** (`src/shared/requesterSubject.js`), in the one shape every use
case publishes them, so the sidebar needs a single renderer. `employee` is read

performs before it acts — and reports five states rather than a blank:
`available`, `not_found` (404, an answer *about the record*), `unavailable`
(403/5xx, the request was never evaluated), `not_looked_up` and
`no_employment_id`. A failed read degrades the block and never the case.

### The fields, and the money line

`full_name`, `job_title`, `status`, `contract_type`, `country_code`.

Two different people read this screen — the customer admin and the payroll
specialist — and both are signing a change to one named person's contract.

- **`status`** is what the approve button will re-check: the write refuses a
  non-active employment and the gate admits only `active`.
- **`job_title`** is the FROM side of the change whenever the amendment touches
  a title.
- **`country_code`** is the single input to
  `/v1/countries/{code}/employment_basic_information`, the schema every gate
  here validates against — and when it is null the gate escalates
  `country_schema_unavailable`, which this row is where a reader can see.
- **`contract_type`** decides which form applies at all.

**Money is on this screen and is not in this block, and the distinction is the
point.** UC-06's decision genuinely turns on salary, so the OLD and NEW values
are published from the amendment row, where they are the subject of the change
and carry their own currency and ×100 scaling. A third pay figure read from a
different record on a different axis would invite a comparison nothing in the
system makes. **Rejected:** `start_date` — no gate reads tenure.

### `requester` on the Zendesk path

`filedBy: "unauthenticated"` is **expected and correct** there and the block
says so rather than implying a defect: a ticket requester is an authenticated
person but not an authenticated company-admin session, which is what this gate
needs.
