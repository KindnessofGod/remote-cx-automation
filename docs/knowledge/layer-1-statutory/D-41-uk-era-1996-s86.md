## Section 86, quoted verbatim — and read subsection by subsection, because the two that matter sit next to each other

> **86 Rights of employer and employee to minimum notice.**
>
> **(1)** The notice required to be given by **an employer** to terminate the
> contract of employment of a person who has been continuously employed for one
> month or more—
> **(a)** is not less than one week's notice if his period of continuous
> employment is less than two years,
> **(b)** is not less than one week's notice **for each year of continuous
> employment** if his period of continuous employment is two years or more but
> less than twelve years, and
> **(c)** is not less than **twelve weeks'** notice if his period of continuous
> employment is twelve years or more.
>
> **(2)** The notice required to be given by **an employee** who has been
> continuously employed for one month or more to terminate his contract of
> employment is **not less than one week**.
>
> **(3)** Any provision for shorter notice in any contract of employment with a
> person who has been continuously employed for one month or more has effect
> subject to subsections (1) and (2); but **this section does not prevent either
> party from waiving his right to notice on any occasion or from accepting a
> payment in lieu of notice**.
>
> **(4)** Any contract of employment of a person who has been continuously
> employed for three months or more which is a contract for a term certain of one
> month or less shall have effect as if it were for an indefinite period; and,
> accordingly, subsections (1) and (2) apply to the contract.
>
> **(5)** … *omitted (1.10.2002) by S.I. 2002/2034, reg. 11, Sch. 2 Pt. 1 para. 3(4)*
>
> **(6)** This section does not affect any right of either party to a contract of
> employment to treat the contract as terminable without notice by reason of the
> conduct of the other party.

One modification is recorded on the face of the section and is worth carrying
because it is the only thing anywhere that *narrows* subsection (2):
**s. 86(2) is excluded (17.11.2021) by the Local Government Act 1972 s. 80B(6)**,
as inserted by the Local Government and Elections (Wales) Act 2021 — a
Welsh-councillor carve-out, irrelevant to an EOR population and named here so a
future reader does not have to rediscover why the timeline has a 2021 entry.

## What this settles for `src/uc05/noticePeriodTable.js`

`NOTICE_PERIOD_TABLE.GB` today carries eight tenure brackets rising from 7 to 84
days, `anchorRule: "continuous"`, and the citation string
*"Employment Rights Act 1996 §86 (sliding scale)"*.

| Question | Answer from s. 86 |
|---|---|
| Is there a sliding scale in s. 86? | **Yes — in subsection (1), and it is the EMPLOYER's.** |
| What does the **employee** owe? | **Not less than one week. Flat. Regardless of tenure**, once one month's continuous employment is passed (s. 86(2)). |
| Is there a tenure dimension on the employee's side at all? | **One, and it is a threshold rather than a ladder**: below one month's continuous employment s. 86 does not engage. |
| Does the citation string name the right subsection? | **No.** It names `§86` without a subsection, and the numbers under it are subsection (1)'s. |

**This is the third instance of the same defect in this table**, after the
Netherlands (**C-14**, BW 7:672's ladder is the employer's, the employee's is a
flat month) and Portugal's probation row (**C-20**, art. 114.º's seven- and
30-day figures are the employer's, the employee's is zero). The United Kingdom
is the first of the three where the wrong party's ladder is **already encoded and
already computing**. Recorded as [`CONTRADICTIONS.md`](CONTRADICTIONS.md)
**C-31**.

Two further things the section supplies that the table has no field for:

1. **s. 86(3) — waiver and payment in lieu.** The one-week floor is not
   absolute: either party may waive notice on any occasion, or accept a payment
   in lieu. So even the corrected figure is a **default the parties may displace
   on the day**, exactly as BW 7:672(8) (**C-14**) and CT art. 400.º(2)
   (**D-02**) are. This table reports a statutory floor as a fixed entitlement
   in all three countries.
2. **s. 86(1)–(2) run on *continuous employment*, a defined term.** Continuity is
   computed under ERA 1996 Part XIV Chapter I (ss. 210–219), not by subtracting
   two dates — the same shape as the Portuguese counting rule in **C-21**. Not
   retrieved by this pass; named so the assumption is visible.

## Note on the arithmetic, for whoever applies C-31

Nobody should port subsection (1)'s ladder into this table — it is the wrong
party's — but it is worth recording that **the encoded copy of it is also wrong
on its own terms**, which is a second, independent reason not to keep it. s. 86(1)(b)
gives **one week per full year** from 2 to 12 years; the table jumps from
`85–120 months → 56 days` to `121+ → 84 days`, so a 10-year employee is told
8 weeks where subsection (1) says 10, and an 11-year employee 12 weeks where it
says 11. The ladder is neither the employee's obligation nor a faithful copy of
the employer's.

## What is still open

- **Northern Ireland is a separate statute book.** ERA 1996 extends to England,
  Wales and Scotland (the section's extent marker reads `E+W+S`); the
  corresponding provision for Northern Ireland is the Employment Rights (Northern
  Ireland) Order 1996, **not retrieved**. `GB` is the ISO code the table uses and
  `GB` excludes Northern Ireland, so the row and the retrieved statute agree on
  scope — but only by accident of the code, and a `UK` row would not.
- **The continuity rules (ss. 210–219) were not retrieved.**
