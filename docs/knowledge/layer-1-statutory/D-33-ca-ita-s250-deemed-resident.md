## The provision

> **250 (1)** For the purposes of this Act, a person shall, subject to
> subsection 250(2), be deemed to have been resident in Canada **throughout a
> taxation year** if the person (a) **sojourned in Canada in the year for a
> period of, or periods the total of which is, 183 days or more**; …

## What this settles

Canada is the **one** demo country in which a 183-day figure genuinely appears
in domestic law — and the window it is measured over is a **taxation year**,
not a rolling 365 days from a trip start. Those two windows give different
answers for the same travel, and the gap is widest for exactly the split-year
trips a workation produces.

Two further features of the text that a day-count gate cannot represent:

- The consequence is **deemed residence throughout the whole year**, not
  residence from the 183rd day. Crossing the line is retroactive to 1 January.
- The word is **"sojourned"**, not "was present". s. 250(1)(a) is a deeming rule
  layered on top of common-law factual residence, not a replacement for it — the
  CRA's administrative view of the latter is D-34, and it is a
  facts-and-circumstances analysis that must never reach a conditional.
